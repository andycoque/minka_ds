## Use something else when

- **The answer is submitted with a form.** `Checkbox`. A switch that only takes
  effect after pressing Save is lying about being immediate.
- **The change is expensive or hard to undo.** A `Button` plus a confirmation, so
  there is somewhere to explain the consequence.
- **There are more than two states.** `Tabs` with `variant="subtle"` for a
  segmented control, or `Select`.

## Do and don't

<DoDont>
  <Rule>
    <Do example={<span className="flex items-center gap-3"><Label htmlFor="sw-a">Notify on failure</Label><Switch id="sw-a" defaultChecked /></span>}>
      Name the setting, so the label reads the same whichever way the switch is
      thrown.
    </Do>
    <Dont example={<span className="flex items-center gap-3"><Label htmlFor="sw-b">Turn on notifications</Label><Switch id="sw-b" /></span>}>
      Write the label as an instruction. It contradicts itself once the switch is
      already on.
    </Dont>
  </Rule>
  <Rule>
    <Do example={<span className="flex items-center gap-3"><Label htmlFor="sw-c">Single use</Label><Switch id="sw-c" /></span>}>
      Let the switch be the whole interaction. Flipping it is the change.
    </Do>
    <Dont example={<span className="flex flex-col items-start gap-2"><span className="flex items-center gap-3"><Label htmlFor="sw-d">Single use</Label><Switch id="sw-d" /></span><Button size="sm">Save</Button></span>}>
      Put a switch behind a Save. The reader has already been told it took effect.
    </Dont>
  </Rule>
</DoDont>

## Props

<PropsTable>
  <Prop name="checked" type="boolean">
    Controlled state. Pair with `onCheckedChange`.
  </Prop>
  <Prop name="onCheckedChange" type="(checked: boolean) => void">
    Fires on change. Do the work here rather than deferring to a Save.
  </Prop>
  <Prop name="defaultChecked" type="boolean" default="false">
    Uncontrolled starting state.
  </Prop>
  <Prop name="disabled" type="boolean" default="false">
    Removes pointer events and drops opacity.
  </Prop>
</PropsTable>
