## Anatomy

<ToastDemo />

Called imperatively from a handler, never rendered as an element. `Toaster` is mounted
once in the app shell and every `toast()` call renders through it, so no page mounts its
own.

The most-used component in the product: 26 call sites.

## Element variations

**The title says what happened, the description says to what.** "Directory key inactive"
with `description: "pay.bancolombia"`. Putting the record's name in the title makes it
long enough to wrap, and the pattern stops being scannable when three toasts stack.

**`toast.success` and `toast.error`** are the two in use. A plain `toast()` exists for
something that is neither, but almost every action either worked or did not.

## Use something else when

- **The reader must acknowledge it.** A dialog. A toast can be missed, so it cannot carry
  anything that requires a decision.
- **The state is on the page.** Show it on the page. A toast saying "3 items selected"
  duplicates what the page already says and then disappears.
- **It is an error the reader must fix.** An inline error on the field, or an `Alert`. A
  validation failure that vanishes after four seconds leaves them stuck.

## Do and don't

<DoDont>
  <Rule>
    <Do>
      Name the outcome in the past tense: "Report queued", "Signer disabled".
    </Do>
    <Dont>
      Say "Success" or "Done". The reader knows something happened; the toast exists to
      say what.
    </Dont>
  </Rule>
  <Rule>
    <Do>
      Put the record's name in `description`.
    </Do>
    <Dont>
      Put it in the title. Long titles wrap and stop being scannable when toasts stack.
    </Dont>
  </Rule>
</DoDont>
