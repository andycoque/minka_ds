## Variants

<AlertVariantsDemo />

Five, and the choice is about what the reader should do rather than how bad the
news is. `warning` is the one that earns its keep: an expiring API key, a bridge
whose retries are running down, a participant registered but not yet funded.

`default` carries no colour, for a message that is neither good nor bad.

## Use something else when

- **It is feedback on an action just taken.** `toast`. An alert persists because
  the condition persists; a toast passes because the moment does.
- **It blocks the reader.** `Dialog`. An alert sits on the page and can be ignored,
  which is wrong if the next step depends on it.
- **It is a field-level error.** The help line under the field, plus
  `aria-invalid`. An alert at the top of a form makes the reader hunt for which
  field is wrong.

## Do and don't

<DoDont>
  <Rule>
    <Do>
      Title the condition and put the remedy in the description: "This key expires
      in 9 days", then what to do about it.
    </Do>
    <Dont>
      Title it "Warning". The variant already carries that, so the line is spent
      saying nothing.
    </Dont>
  </Rule>
  <Rule>
    <Do>
      Put the action in the alert when there is one, e.g. a Rotate button beside
      the text.
    </Do>
    <Dont>
      Describe a problem and leave the reader to find the fix somewhere else on the
      page.
    </Dont>
  </Rule>
</DoDont>
