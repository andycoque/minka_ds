The default trigger is a circular question mark beside a heading, which is where all
seven studio usages put it.

## Variants

Two modes, and the choice is about what is around the trigger.

`popover` floats the card beside the trigger and is portaled, so it works anywhere,
including inside a dialog or a table cell. Use it unless you have a reason not to.

`inset` expands the card inside the nearest positioned container and frosts what is
behind it. It suits a panel with empty space to fill, where a floating card would
cover content the reader still wants.

## Use something else when

- **It is one line.** `Tooltip`. Opening a card to read six words is a poor trade.
- **The reader needs it to finish the task.** Put it on the page. Anything behind a
  trigger is optional by construction.
- **It is a whole section that collapses.** `ExpandablePanel`.

## Do and don't

<DoDont>
  <Rule>
    <Do>
      Explain a concept the reader might not share: what quorum means, why a
      period has a limit.
    </Do>
    <Dont>
      Restate the label. If the field name needs explaining, fix the field name.
    </Dont>
  </Rule>
  <Rule>
    <Do>
      Keep `popover` unless the surrounding panel has space that wants filling.
    </Do>
    <Dont>
      Use `inset` inside a dialog or a table. It needs a positioned container with
      room, and neither reliably has one.
    </Dont>
  </Rule>
</DoDont>

## Props

<PropsTable>
  <Prop name="title" type="string">
    The card heading.
  </Prop>
  <Prop name="children" type="ReactNode">
    The explanation.
  </Prop>
  <Prop name="mode" type="'popover' | 'inset'" default="'popover'">
    See [Variants](#variants).
  </Prop>
  <Prop name="trigger" type="{ icon?: ReactNode; label?: string }">
    Overrides the default question mark. A `label` makes it a text trigger.
  </Prop>
</PropsTable>
