## Anatomy

<KbdDemo />

One `Kbd` is one thing to press. A modifier plus a letter is one thing to press, so
`⌘K` is a single chip rather than two side by side.

That is what `SearchBar` does with `kbdHint`: it wraps whatever you pass in a single
`Kbd`, and every search bar in the product passes the modifier glyph followed by the
letter.

## Element variations

The modifier is a **glyph on macOS and a word on Windows**, so it is passed by the
caller rather than hardcoded. `usePlatform` reports which one to use, and the same
call appears at every search bar:

<Specimen>
  <span className="flex items-center gap-4">
    <Kbd><Command className="size-3" /> K</Kbd>
    <Kbd>Ctrl K</Kbd>
  </span>
</Specimen>

A key with no modifier is the glyph alone, as in the transactions search dropdown
telling the reader what Enter will do:

<Specimen>
  <Kbd><CornerDownLeft className="size-3" /></Kbd>
</Specimen>

## Use something else when

- **It is a code or an identifier.** Inline code, or `DataCell` in a table. `Kbd`
  says "press this".
- **It is a menu shortcut hint.** `DropdownMenuShortcut`, which aligns it to the
  right of the item for you.
- **It is a search field's shortcut.** Pass `kbdHint` to `SearchBar` instead of
  placing a `Kbd` yourself. It already puts one in the field's trailing addon.

## Do and don't

<DoDont>
  <Rule>
    <Do example={<Kbd><Command className="size-3" /> K</Kbd>}>
      Keep a modifier and its key in one chip. It is one gesture, and it matches every
      search bar in the product.
    </Do>
    {/* The gap is deliberately the DS default here — two chips at any spacing read as
        two separate presses, which is the mistake being shown. */}
    <Dont example={<span className="flex items-center gap-1"><Kbd><Command className="size-3" /></Kbd><Kbd>K</Kbd></span>}>
      Split it into two chips. It reads as two keys pressed one after the other.
    </Dont>
  </Rule>
</DoDont>
